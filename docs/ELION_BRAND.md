# ELION Brand

## Name

ELION

## Pronunciation

EE-lee-on

## Brand philosophy

ELION blends futuristic technology, human purpose, and subtle spiritual depth. The brand should feel advanced, credible, and humane rather than purely corporate or purely abstract. It communicates engineering capability while remaining rooted in compassion, dignity, and hope.

## Official logo

The official ELION logo is a futuristic metallic wordmark. The letters E, L, O, and N form the main technological structure. The I subtly incorporates a Marian figure. A star sits above her head. A single orbital trajectory crosses the star and wraps around the wordmark. Mary's cloth carries a restrained warm-gold tone while the primary letter structure remains metallic.

The logo must remain subtle and legible as “ELION” at normal viewing distance. It should feel advanced and symbolic without becoming ornate or distracting. The spiritual element should be quiet, respectful, and integrated into the design rather than emphasized as a slogan or emblem.

## Primary colors

- Metallic silver / graphite tone for the core brand structure
- Warm gold for the subtle spiritual accent
- Deep space navy or dark cosmic blue for contrast and trust

## Secondary colors

- Clean white
- Off-white or pale stone
- Subtle blue-grey tones
- High-contrast accent colors used sparingly for interface emphasis

## Typography direction

Typography should feel technical, modern, and precise. Favor geometric or contemporary sans-serif styles with strong legibility and clear engineering tone. The brand should avoid overly playful or decorative lettering.

## Spacing

Spacing should be disciplined and intentional. The logo and wordmark must maintain generous breathing room, clear hierarchy, and strong readability. Overcrowding should be avoided.

## Animation philosophy

Motion should be subtle, purposeful, and elegant. Animations should imply motion, orbit, precision, and confidence without becoming loud, flashy, or distracting. The orbital motif should be used sparingly and intentionally.

## What not to do

- Do not reshape or reinterpret the official logo in a way that changes its meaning or recognizability.
- Do not make the logo overly ornate, cartoonish, or decorative.
- Do not introduce aggressive or flashy visual treatments that undermine the brand's serious purpose.
- Do not dilute the symbol with excessive religious iconography or ornamental styling.
- Do not replace the core visual system with generic tech branding.

The official logo and brand system are a source of truth for the project. Design decisions should support clarity, integrity, and a long-term sense of purpose.
